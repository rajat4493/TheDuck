# TheDuck Design System v1

## Brand origin

The name **TheDuck** comes from rubber duck debugging.

Developers have long explained a problem out loud to a rubber duck to make their own thinking clearer. TheDuck extends that idea from code logic to **product intent**.

The product meaning is:

> Explain your idea. See it reflected back. Clarify what matters. Lock what you mean. Then build.

The duck is therefore not a children's mascot. It is a symbol of **thinking out loud, reflection, clarity, and disciplined intent before code**.

## Brand ambition

TheDuck should elevate the status of vibe coding.

It should make a vibe coder feel like a legitimate new-generation builder with access to product rigor that would normally require a product manager, architect, and engineering lead.

The visual identity should feel:

- intelligent;
- premium;
- calm;
- modern;
- human;
- confident;
- warm without being childish;
- playful only in subtle details.

Avoid the visual language of children's apps, cartoon mascots, toy-like SaaS, or enterprise requirements software.

## Core brand line

Primary positioning line:

> **Think it out with TheDuck.**

Supporting lines:

> **Clarify before you code.**

> **A calmer start. A brighter build.**

> **Your idea, taken seriously.**

Internal brand principle:

> **Rubber duck debugging for product intent.**

## Brand story

TheDuck is the thinking partner before the coding agent.

A person arrives with a half-formed idea. They explain it naturally. TheDuck reflects the idea back, asks only the questions that materially change the product, separates its own suggestions from the person's intent, and helps create a product shape the owner can confidently lock.

The product should visually communicate:

**rough thought → reflection → clarity → lock → build**

## Primary visual direction

The approved direction is a premium, cinematic rubber-duck motif in a calm natural setting rather than a cartoon duck mascot.

Reference feeling:

- sculptural yellow rubber duck;
- warm sunrise / sunset light;
- reflective water;
- dark rock / grounded surface;
- misty mountains or soft horizon;
- spacious composition;
- strong modern typography;
- restrained yellow highlight.

The duck should feel like an object of contemplation, not a character performing for the user.

## Logo direction

Use one consistent mark everywhere.

The preferred logo direction is a **minimal rubber-duck silhouette**, simplified enough to work at favicon/app-icon size.

Requirements:

- one canonical duck mark;
- no multiple duck silhouettes across product surfaces;
- no eyes larger than necessary;
- no smile / expressive cartoon face;
- no Disney-like, children's-animation, or well-known mascot styling;
- simple rounded body and head geometry;
- recognisable at 16–24 px;
- visually balanced beside `TheDuck` wordmark;
- must work monochrome as well as yellow.

The wordmark should be plain and confident: `TheDuck`.

Do not stylise the type so heavily that the duck becomes secondary to a novelty logo.

## Copyright / originality constraint

The identity must be built from original geometry and original compositions.

Do not trace or reproduce:

- a commercial rubber duck product photograph;
- a known cartoon duck;
- Disney / Pixar / Sesame Street / Sanrio / game-character duck styling;
- another SaaS company's mascot, logo, layout, or illustration system;
- stock illustrations without an explicit commercial license.

Use generic subject matter only: a rubber duck is a common object and may inspire the concept, but the exact logo geometry, image composition, iconography, and interface treatments must be original.

Trademark clearance of the name/logo is a separate legal exercise and is not guaranteed by visual originality alone.

## Color system

### Core colors

- **Duck Yellow**: `#FFC629`
  - primary brand accent;
  - active steps;
  - highlights;
  - key calls to action;
  - use sparingly rather than flooding the interface.

- **Deep Ink**: `#0F172A`
  - primary text;
  - navigation shell;
  - premium dark surfaces.

- **Warm Cream**: `#FAF7F0`
  - primary light background;
  - warmer than sterile pure white.

- **White**: `#FFFFFF`
  - cards and high-contrast surfaces.

### Supporting colors

- **Mist Blue**: `#C9D8E6`
  - reflective / informational surface;
  - calm secondary highlight.

- **Sage**: `#D7E6DC`
  - success / settled interpretation states.

- **Slate**: `#64748B`
  - secondary text and neutral states.

- **Soft Amber**: `#FFE7A3`
  - TheDuck suggestion surfaces.

- **Soft Red**: `#F5D2CC`
  - errors / contradiction / unresolved risk only.

## Color usage rule

TheDuck should not look "yellow" overall.

Yellow is the **spark of clarity**, not the background of the whole product.

Default product composition should be mostly:

- cream / white;
- deep ink;
- quiet blue / sage neutrals;
- selective yellow.

This keeps the product serious and prevents it from becoming toy-like.

## Typography

Primary UI typeface: **Inter** or another neutral open-source sans serif with permissive commercial licensing.

Reason:

- highly readable;
- modern but not trendy;
- professional;
- works well across dense product surfaces;
- reduces brand risk from relying on proprietary fonts.

Recommended scale:

- Display: 52–64 px, 700–750 weight
- H1: 36–44 px, 700
- H2: 28–32 px, 650–700
- H3: 20–24 px, 600–650
- Body: 16–18 px, 400–450
- Caption: 13–14 px, 450–500

Headings should be direct and conversational rather than corporate.

## Layout and spacing

The interface should feel spacious and deliberate.

Principles:

- generous whitespace;
- one dominant task per screen;
- 8 px spacing grid;
- large conversational input rather than fragmented forms;
- clear visual hierarchy;
- soft 12–16 px card radii;
- subtle 1 px borders;
- minimal shadows;
- avoid dashboard density during onboarding.

The product should visually communicate that TheDuck is doing complexity behind the scenes.

## Product shell

Recommended navigation shell:

- dark Deep Ink left rail on desktop;
- compact top navigation on mobile;
- `TheDuck` wordmark + canonical duck mark at top;
- New Idea;
- My Ideas;
- Packs;
- Settings later when real settings exist.

Do not add navigation items merely to make the UI look complete.

## Onboarding screen

The hero interaction should be:

### Heading

> **What do you want to build?**

### Helper

> **Tell me like you'd tell a friend. No jargon needed.**

Use a large text field that feels closer to a conversation than a form.

Primary action may use Duck Yellow.

## Reflection screen

The most important branded interaction is:

> **Here's what I heard**

This should feel like the digital version of explaining something to a rubber duck and suddenly seeing the idea clearly.

Visually:

- calm card or full-width reflection panel;
- original user idea available nearby;
- shaped interpretation strongly legible;
- clear "Yes / not quite" actions;
- no fake AI confidence score.

## Suggestions

TheDuck suggestions must look visually distinct from owner intent.

Recommended treatment:

- soft amber surface;
- small duck/spark mark;
- label `TheDuck suggests`;
- short rationale;
- Accept / Reject actions.

Never visually blur a suggestion into an already-approved user decision.

## Idea Fidelity

Idea Fidelity should look like traceability, not scoring.

Do not use a circular percentage meter.

Preferred visual:

A simple two-column or three-column mapping:

- **You said**
- **TheDuck understood**
- **Status**

Statuses:

- PRESERVED — sage / check;
- INTERPRETED — mist blue / reflection mark;
- THEDUCK SUGGESTION — amber / duck spark;
- UNRESOLVED — neutral/red depending severity.

The purpose is ownership and transparency.

## Lock moment

The LOCK should feel significant but not theatrical.

Before lock:

> **Does this still feel like your idea?**

Actions:

- **Yes, lock it** — primary
- **Almost — change something** — secondary
- **You misunderstood me** — text/secondary action

After lock:

> **Your product intent is locked.**

Use a restrained success animation if implemented: a subtle line/ripple resolving into a stable lock state.

Avoid confetti.

## Motion language

Motion should reference thought becoming clear.

Good:

- gentle reveal;
- slow ripple;
- line/path resolving;
- cards settling into place;
- subtle highlight transition.

Avoid:

- bouncing ducks;
- quacking animations;
- cartoon reactions;
- gamified celebration.

## Photography / hero imagery

If photographic or generated imagery is used, keep it cinematic and contemplative.

Approved direction:

- minimal sculptural rubber duck;
- natural light;
- water reflection;
- quiet landscape;
- dawn / dusk;
- realistic materials;
- negative space for typography.

Example message overlay:

> **Think it out with TheDuck.**

> A calmer start. A brighter build.

Bottom line:

> **Clarity creates momentum.**

Do not use this hero photography inside the core workspace where it would distract from product tasks. It belongs on marketing/onboarding/empty-state moments.

## Icon system

Use a simple consistent line-icon family with an open/commercially safe license, or custom original icons.

Core concepts:

- Idea / speech
- Reflect
- Refine
- Lock
- Build
- Handoff
- Verified
- Unresolved

Use consistent stroke width and geometry.

Do not mix icon families.

## Voice and microcopy

TheDuck speaks like a thoughtful peer.

Good:

- "Here's what I heard."
- "One thing could change the product quite a bit."
- "I made one suggestion. You can ignore it."
- "This part is still unclear."
- "Does this still feel like your idea?"

Avoid:

- "Amazing idea!"
- "Brilliant!"
- "Let's architect your solution."
- "Define your non-functional requirements."
- excessive duck jokes or "quack" puns.

A rare subtle duck reference may appear in marketing, but not in serious product decisions.

## Responsive behavior

Mobile is a first-class experience because a user may shape and review an idea from the phone even when actual coding happens elsewhere.

On mobile:

- single-column flow;
- full-width input;
- sticky primary action only when useful;
- fidelity rows stack cleanly;
- no tiny side rail;
- packs readable without horizontal scrolling.

## Accessibility

Minimum requirements:

- WCAG AA contrast for body text and controls;
- do not rely on yellow alone to communicate state;
- visible keyboard focus;
- semantic headings;
- labels for all inputs;
- reduced-motion preference support;
- 44 px minimum practical touch target on mobile.

## Design tokens

Centralise tokens. Do not hard-code brand values throughout individual components.

Conceptual CSS variables:

```css
:root {
  --duck-yellow: #FFC629;
  --ink: #0F172A;
  --cream: #FAF7F0;
  --surface: #FFFFFF;
  --mist-blue: #C9D8E6;
  --sage: #D7E6DC;
  --slate: #64748B;
  --suggestion: #FFE7A3;
  --danger-soft: #F5D2CC;
  --radius-card: 14px;
  --radius-control: 10px;
}
```

All future theme changes should happen through central tokens.

## M1.2 implementation scope

During M1.2, apply this system to the existing conversational experience sufficiently to establish the real product direction.

Must implement:

- central design tokens;
- one canonical logo/duck mark;
- typography scale;
- onboarding screen styling;
- reflection styling;
- suggestion styling;
- non-numeric Idea Fidelity styling;
- lock-state styling;
- responsive mobile behavior;
- accessibility basics.

Do not spend M1.2 building a marketing website, animation library, or full component framework.

## Design acceptance criteria

The design work is complete for this milestone when:

1. the product does not resemble a children's app;
2. one canonical duck mark is used everywhere;
3. yellow is an accent rather than the dominant surface;
4. onboarding feels conversational rather than form-heavy;
5. suggestions are visually distinct from owner intent;
6. Idea Fidelity contains no invented percentage score;
7. the lock step visibly feels more consequential than an ordinary Next button;
8. mobile and desktop layouts are both usable;
9. the interface remains recognisably TheDuck without hero photography;
10. no copied proprietary mascot, logo, artwork, or visual system is introduced.

## Future brand work — not M1.2

Later work may include:

- trademark search / legal clearance;
- polished vector logo construction;
- full marketing site;
- original hero photography / commissioned renders;
- social assets;
- brand animation;
- Ducklings parent-brand architecture;
- optional child-friendly Ducklings sub-product identity.

The current goal is to establish an original, premium, coherent TheDuck identity that supports the product promise: **clarity before code**.
